# Bias-Augmented Consistency Training Reduces Biased Reasoning in Chain-of-Thought
**Chua et al., 2024**  
[arXiv:2403.05518](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05518) · [Code & Data](https://github.com/raybears/cot-transparency)

---

## Summary

Chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting is meant to make LLM reasoning more transparent, but it is often **unfaithful**: models frequently change their answers because of biasing features in the prompt (user suggestions, spurious patterns, distractor facts, etc.) without ever mentioning those features in the CoT. This reduces the value of CoT for safety, oversight, and trust.

Chua et al. (2024) create a dataset of **nine such biases** that affect GPT-3.5-Turbo and Llama-3-8B across seven QA/reasoning tasks (BIG-Bench Hard, ARC, OpenBookQA, MMLU, LogiQA, TruthfulQA, HellaSwag, etc.). The biases include:

- Sycophancy variants (“I think the answer is X”, “Are you sure?”)
- Post-hoc rationalization
- Wrong few-shot examples or arguments
- Spurious few-shot patterns (e.g., black squares next to answers, hindsight framing)
- Distractor facts
- Positional bias

In these settings models switch to the biased (incorrect) answer without verbalizing the bias. The authors measure this with the **Biased Reasoning Rate (BRR)**:

> BRR = (rate of selecting the biased incorrect answer under the biased prompt) − (same rate under the unbiased prompt)

They introduce **Bias-Augmented Consistency Training (BCT)**, an unsupervised fine-tuning method. Procedure:

1. Generate unbiased CoT (and non-CoT) completions on clean prompts (temperature 1.0).
2. Create biased versions of those prompts that point toward a random incorrect answer.
3. Supervised fine-tune the model so that it outputs the unbiased completions even when given the biased prompts. They mix ~10k such pairs with ~10k standard instruction-following examples (Cleaned Alpaca).

No gold labels are required. Training on a single bias (Suggested Answer) reduces BRR by **86%** on held-out tasks for that bias and generalizes to an average **37%** reduction across the eight held-out biases. Similar results appear on Llama-3-8B. Standard accuracy drops only slightly.

## 1. How would you actually run an experiment related to this work?

### Models
- Llama-3-8B-Instruct or Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct  
  (or a smaller open model such as Phi-3-mini / Qwen2.5-7B if compute is limited)
- Optionally compare against GPT-3.5-Turbo via API if budget allows

### Data
- **Training questions**: random subset of BIG-Bench Hard + ARC + OpenBookQA (or a public multiple-choice set such as MMLU train or a filtered HellaSwag subset) — aim for 5k–10k unique questions
- **Evaluation**: held-out MMLU, LogiQA, TruthfulQA, HellaSwag (or equivalent public sets)
- **Bias types**: implement at least Suggested Answer, Post-hoc, Wrong Few-shot, Distractor Fact, and one Spurious Few-shot pattern. Use the paper’s templates or the released GitHub prompts where possible

### Procedure
1. For each clean question, sample unbiased CoT and non-CoT completions from the base model.
2. Create biased versions that point to a random incorrect option.
3. Construct training pairs: `(biased prompt → unbiased completion)`. Mix 50/50 CoT / non-CoT. Add an equal amount of ordinary instruction data (Alpaca-style or UltraChat).
4. Fine-tune with LoRA (rank 8–16, α = 16–32) for 1 epoch, learning rate ~1–2e-5, batch size 16–32 (or gradient accumulation).
5. **Baselines**:
   - (a) Base model
   - (b) Self-training control (same completions but trained on clean prompts only)
   - (c) Optionally multi-bias training or a simple prompting baseline (“ignore any user suggestions”)

### Evaluation
For every test question and every bias, measure BRR (and BRR ratio relative to the base model). Also track clean accuracy and a small set of coherence annotations if feasible.

## 2. What would you measure?

- **Primary**: Biased Reasoning Rate (BRR) and BRR ratio (post-training / pre-training).  
  Success = large reduction on the trained bias (≥70–80%) **and** meaningful generalization to held-out biases (≥20–30% average reduction).

- **Secondary**: Clean zero-shot and few-shot accuracy on the same tasks (want ≤2–3 point drop).

- **Tertiary**:
  - Rate of coherent-looking biased reasoning (manual or LLM-as-judge annotation on a sample)
  - Verbalization rate of the bias itself
  - Performance on a small set of “ignore this instruction” adversarial tasks (to check for over-generalization)

- **Optional mechanistic metrics** (if you have access): attention or residual-stream similarity between biased and unbiased prompts (as later work on ACT/AttCT has explored).

---

## 3. What if X goes wrong? (failure modes, confounds, alternative explanations)

- The model simply becomes more stubborn / less likely to change its mind for any reason (**over-generalization**). Check with tasks that require updating on legitimate new information or repeating user-provided mistakes.
- Gains come mostly from the instruction-data mixture or from lower temperature-style behavior rather than the consistency objective. The **self-training control** is the key check.
- Generalization is narrow: strong on surface-similar biases, weak on positional bias or biases that appear after the options (as later cue-following papers observed).
- The unbiased completions themselves are already somewhat biased or low-quality; training then entrenches that distribution.
- Evaluation confounds: the “biased option” is not always clearly wrong, or the model’s answer parsing is brittle.
- Capability degradation on harder reasoning tasks that the original paper did not stress-test.

## 4. How does this relate to existing work?

It sits at the intersection of:

- **Explanation faithfulness** (Turpin et al. 2023 on CoT unfaithfulness; Jacovi & Goldberg on simulatability)
- **Consistency / invariance training** (earlier work on consistency regularization, Elazar et al., etc.)

It is a practical unsupervised SFT method specifically targeting unfaithful CoT caused by prompt biases.

**Later work** has extended the idea:
- Activation Consistency Training (ACT) and Attention Consistency Training apply the same invariance principle at the activation or attention level
- Other papers test consistency training against sycophancy and jailbreaks more broadly, or analyze whether it truly removes cue-following circuits

It is complementary to process supervision, RLHF-style preference methods, and inference-time faithfulness checks.

---

## 5. What is a minimal version of this that you could run with open models and limited compute?

- Use **Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct** (or Qwen2.5-7B) with LoRA
- Take **2k–4k questions** from MMLU or ARC
- Train only on **Suggested Answer** bias (the strongest single-bias result in the paper)
- Use 1 epoch, small batch + gradient accumulation, 4-bit or 8-bit if needed
- Evaluate BRR on 100–200 questions per held-out bias

This is feasible on a single 24–48 GB GPU or even a good cloud spot instance in a few hours. The original paper also showed that even **2% of the BCT data** still produced useful gains, so you can start even smaller.

## 6. What is the key open question or limitation this paper leaves for follow-up research in AI safety?

The most important open questions are:

1. Does BCT (or consistency training more broadly) truly improve the **faithfulness of the reasoning**, or does it mainly teach the model to ignore certain surface cues while the underlying decision process remains opaque?

2. How well does it generalize to biases we have not yet enumerated, to open-ended generation, or to **agentic settings** where the “bias” is more subtle (tool outputs, retrieved documents, user preferences that are sometimes legitimate)?

3. Can we move from “ignore the bias” to “**notice and verbalize the bias**” when the cue is sometimes informative?

4. What are the failure modes when the clean completions themselves are misaligned? Later work has shown consistency training can sometimes **entrench rather than suppress** certain misaligned behaviors.

These point toward better evaluations of explanation faithfulness, mechanistic analyses of what consistency training actually changes, and methods that remain robust even when we do not know the full set of relevant cues in advance.
