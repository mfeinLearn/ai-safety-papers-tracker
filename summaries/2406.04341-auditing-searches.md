# Interpreting the Second-Order Effects of Neurons in CLIP

**Authors:** Yossi Gandelsman, Alexei A. Efros, Jacob Steinhardt (UC Berkeley)  
**arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04341  
**Project page:** https://yossigandelsman.github.io/clip_neurons/

## Summary

The paper develops an automated method to interpret individual neurons in CLIP’s vision transformer. Direct (first-order) effects through the residual stream are negligible, and indirect effects measured by mean-ablation are confounded by self-repair and redundancy.  

They introduce the **second-order lens**: the contribution of a neuron that flows through all subsequent multi-head attention layers to the final image-text representation. Empirically these effects are:

- Concentrated in late MLP layers
- Highly selective (<2% of images per neuron)
- Well approximated by a single direction in CLIP’s joint text-image space

Sparse decomposition of these directions into text embeddings reveals strong **polysemanticity** (neurons respond to multiple unrelated concepts, e.g. ships and cars).  

This interpretation is then used for two applications:

1. **Semantic adversarial examples** (the red-teaming component): Mine the overlapping concepts from polysemantic neurons, generate captions that combine a source class with those spurious concepts, and produce images that fool CLIP at rates substantially higher than baselines.
2. **Zero-shot segmentation**: Aggregate the spatial activation maps of the most aligned neurons to obtain competitive segmentation masks.

**Core claim:** Automated neuron interpretation via second-order effects can both expose model vulnerabilities and unlock new capabilities.

## 1. How would you actually run an experiment related to this work?

### Concrete setup for the red-teaming / semantic-adversary pipeline

- **Models**
  - CLIP: OpenAI ViT-B-32 (or ViT-L-14)
  - Text-to-image: DeepFloyd IF, Stable Diffusion 3, or Flux
  - LLM for caption generation: LLaMA-3-8B / Qwen2.5

- **Data**
  - ~5k ImageNet training images for effect computation and PCA
  - CIFAR-10 (or ImageNet) class pairs for attack evaluation
  - Text pool: 30k common English words + optional LLM-generated class descriptions

- **Procedure**
  1. Compute second-order effects \(\phi_n^l(I)\) for late-layer neurons.
  2. PCA on the high-norm effects of each neuron → direction \(r_n^l\).
  3. Sparse-decompose \(r_n^l\) with orthogonal matching pursuit into the text pool.
  4. For pair \((c_1, c_2)\):
     - Select top-\(k\) neurons most aligned with \(v = \text{embed}(c_2) - \text{embed}(c_1)\).
     - Score phrases by weighted contribution across those neurons.
     - Prompt LLM to write descriptions of \(c_1\) that include the top phrases (explicitly exclude \(c_2\) and synonyms).
     - Generate images from the filtered descriptions.
  5. Measure zero-shot binary classification accuracy of CLIP on the generated images.

- **Baselines**
  - Random neurons
  - Neurons ranked by indirect-effect magnitude
  - Top similar words to \(v\) without the neuron pipeline
  - Random captions

---

## 2. What would you measure?

- **Primary**: Attack success rate (ASR) — fraction of images for which CLIP prefers the wrong class.
- Selectivity statistics of second-order effects (% of images with large norm).
- Reconstruction fidelity: zero-shot ImageNet accuracy after replacing true effects with the rank-1 + sparse-text approximation.
- Qualitative: attribution maps on adversarial images; human verification that spurious concepts are visible and causal.
- For segmentation experiments: pixel accuracy, mIoU, mAP.

Success criterion: ASR meaningfully higher than the strongest baseline (paper reports ~3–7× gains on several pairs).

---

## 3. What if X goes wrong? (failure modes & confounds)

- LLM generates incoherent or incomplete descriptions.
- Text-to-image model drops the intended spurious concepts.
- Unmodeled neuron–neuron or neuron–attention interactions cancel the intended effect.
- Self-repair compensates when the image is actually shown.
- Spurious correlations are real dataset shortcuts rather than pure model artifacts.
- Rank-1 approximation is poor for some neurons, making the decomposition misleading.
- Possible data leakage between the text pool / LLM training data and evaluation images.

---

## 4. How does this relate to existing work?

- Mechanistic interpretability of vision models and of CLIP attention heads (authors’ prior work).
- Polysemanticity and superposition (Elhage et al.).
- Dictionary learning / sparse autoencoders on neurons.
- Semantic / concept-level adversarial attacks (vs. \(\ell_p\)-bounded pixel attacks).
- CLIP-based zero-shot segmentation (Chefer et al., TextSpan, etc.).

It is one of the clearer demonstrations that automated neuron-level interpretation can be turned into a practical red-teaming tool for vision-language models.

## 5. Minimal version with open models and limited compute

- Use CLIP ViT-B-32.
- Compute second-order effects only on layers 9–10 and only on 500–1000 images.
- Skip full OMP; take top-5 text directions by cosine similarity per neuron.
- Pick one class pair (e.g. dog vs cat).
- Manually or with a small LLM write ~10–20 captions mixing the source class with the top spurious concepts.
- Generate 20–50 images with any free text-to-image model and measure ASR.
- Fits on a single high-end consumer GPU or one A100 in a few hours.

---

## 6. Key open question / limitation for AI safety follow-up

The paper shows that polysemantic neurons create systematically exploitable spurious correlations, but it does not explain *why* particular concepts are bound together, nor does it model higher-order interactions among neurons or their effects on attention patterns.

**Safety-relevant open question:**  
Can this style of automated interpretation be used to *discover and mitigate* the most dangerous spurious correlations (or latent capabilities) *before* deployment, rather than only generating attacks after the fact? How well do the same techniques transfer to larger multimodal models or to models that have undergone adversarial training or safety tuning?
