# Towards Consistent Natural-Language Explanations via Explanation-Consistency Finetuning

**Paper:** Chen et al., 2024  
**arXiv:** [2401.13986](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13986)

---

## Summary

LLMs generate fluent, convincing natural-language explanations, but these explanations are often **inconsistent** across related inputs. Classic example: the model explains “all birds can fly” for “Can sparrows fly?” yet answers “no” to “Can penguins fly?”. Inconsistent explanations make it hard for a human to form a reliable mental model of the model’s behavior (i.e., to simulate what the model would do on related cases).

The authors propose **explanation-consistency finetuning (EC-finetuning)**. The method works as follows:

1. Start with existing (question, explanation) pairs.
2. Use a strong LLM (GPT-4) to generate related follow-up questions that should be answerable from the original explanation.
3. Use a second LLM (Claude-2) to produce answers + explanations for those follow-ups that are *forced to be consistent* with the original explanation.
4. Finetune the target model (LLaMA-2-13B) on this synthetic dataset of consistent explanation pairs.

They evaluate on four finetuning datasets (StrategyQA, MedQA variants, MedMCQA) and seven out-of-distribution datasets (BoolQ, NQ, OBQA, ARC-Easy, PubMedQA, etc.), all cast as yes/no QA.

**Key results:**
- +10.0% relative improvement in explanation consistency on the finetuning datasets.
- +4.5% relative generalization to unseen datasets.
- Modest accuracy gains (~5% relative).
- Consistency is measured as the fraction of follow-up answers that match what a human (simulated by an LLM) would expect given the original explanation (counterfactual simulatability precision from their prior work).

## 1. How would you actually run an experiment related to this work?

**Concrete setup:**

- **Target model**: LLaMA-2-13B (or a stronger open model such as LLaMA-3-8B/70B-Instruct or Mistral).
- **Synthetic data generation**:
  - Take a seed set of (question, explanation) pairs from StrategyQA or a medical QA set.
  - Prompt GPT-4 (or Claude-3.5 / a strong open model) to generate 3–5 related follow-up questions per seed example that are answerable from the explanation.
  - Prompt a second model to answer those follow-ups while being explicitly instructed to stay consistent with the original explanation.
- **Finetuning**: Supervised finetuning on the augmented data (original + synthetic consistent pairs) using standard LoRA or full finetuning.
- **Baselines**: Standard supervised finetuning on the original (non-augmented) data; zero-shot / few-shot; optionally a “same-model” ablation where one model both generates and answers the follow-ups.
- **Evaluation**: Hold out test sets from the finetuning domains + several OOD yes/no QA datasets. Generate explanations on the test set, then measure consistency on both related and rephrased follow-ups.

---

## 2. What would you measure?

- **Primary metric – Explanation consistency**: Fraction of follow-up questions for which the model’s answer matches the answer a human (or strong LLM simulator) would expect given the original explanation. Report separately for *related* questions and *rephrased* questions.
- **Task accuracy**: Yes/no accuracy on the original questions.
- **Secondary**: Correlation between consistency and accuracy; consistency on correct vs. incorrect predictions; human evaluation on a small subset to validate the LLM-based consistency metric.
- **Success criteria**: Statistically significant relative gains in consistency (≥5–10%) on both in-domain and OOD sets, with no large accuracy regression.

---

## 3. What if X goes wrong? (failure modes, confounds, alternative explanations)

- **Synthetic data quality is poor**: The generated follow-ups are not truly constrained by the original explanation, or the second model fails to stay consistent → finetuning teaches noise or even increases inconsistency.
- **Self-bias / mode collapse**: Using the same model family for data generation and finetuning can amplify existing inconsistencies or reduce diversity.
- **Accuracy–consistency tradeoff**: Gains in consistency come at the cost of accuracy (observed in their single-LLM ablation).
- **Metric is circular**: Consistency is judged by another LLM; improvements may reflect better style-matching rather than genuine logical consistency.
- **Limited to yes/no**: The method and metric may not transfer to open-ended or multi-step reasoning tasks.
- **Overfitting to the synthetic distribution**: Strong in-domain gains that fail to generalize.

---

## 4. How does this relate to existing work?

- Builds directly on the authors’ prior work on **counterfactual simulatability** of explanations (Chen et al., 2023), which defined the consistency metric used here.
- Related to (but distinct from) CoT faithfulness literature (Turpin et al. 2023, Lanham et al. 2023, Radhakrishnan et al. 2023). Those papers focus on whether the *stated reasoning steps* causally drive the answer. This paper focuses on whether *natural-language explanations are logically consistent across related inputs*.
- Complements process-supervision and self-consistency methods, and is orthogonal to work that improves faithfulness via decomposition or process rewards.
- Also connects to broader efforts on controllable generation and learning from explanations (Stiennon et al., RLHF-style work), but specifically targets cross-example consistency rather than preference or helpfulness.

## 5. Minimal version with open models and limited compute

- Use **LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct** (or even 7B) as both the target and the data-generation model.
- Seed data: 200–500 examples from StrategyQA or a public medical QA set.
- Generate only 1–2 follow-ups per example with the same model (cheaper, though weaker than the two-model setup).
- Finetune with LoRA (rank 8–16) for 1–2 epochs on a single 24–48 GB GPU or via a cheap API.
- Evaluate consistency on 50–100 test examples using the same model as the “human simulator.”
- This is enough to check whether the qualitative improvement appears, at the cost of smaller absolute gains than the original paper.

---

## 6. Key open question / limitation for AI safety follow-up

The paper improves *consistency* of explanations, but consistency ≠ faithfulness or truthfulness. A model can learn to produce explanations that are mutually consistent yet systematically wrong or unfaithful to its internal process. For safety, the open question is whether consistency-trained explanations can be made to also be *faithful* (i.e., actually reflect the model’s decision process) and whether they can surface misaligned behavior (sycophancy, deception, reward hacking) rather than merely paper over it with coherent-sounding text. A second limitation is that the method currently relies on strong external models to generate the synthetic data; it is unclear how well it scales when only weaker models are available or when the target model is already highly capable.
